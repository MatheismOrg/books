## What Did the Money Build?

Thomas Sowell checked what government programs did to the people they were meant to help. He was a Marxist until 1960, when a summer job at the Labor Department had him study the minimum wage in Puerto Rico. California's homelessness money burned. The state spent $24 billion on homelessness in five years, the state auditor found it had not tracked whether any of it worked, and the homeless count rose by about a fifth. Los Angeles paid up to $837,000 to build a single apartment for a homeless person. Agencies and nonprofits get more money when there are more homeless people, so the problem pays them to grow. Why shut down a grift that works? Grow it.

Pay for results instead. Let competing providers bid to shelter the homeless in a decent building with security, nurses and teachers, and pay them for each person who leaves a year later sober, employed and housed. The same money would build instead of burn.

Health care burns money when someone else pays. Americans spent 18 percent of their economy on health care in 2024, mostly through insurers and government. Insurers check doctors' prices, but patients are disconnected from them and from the limits their real needs and budgets would normally set. A copay puts part of the price back on the patient. Singapore goes further: every worker saves into a personal health account, Medisave, and patients spend that money as their own. It spent 4.5 percent of its economy on health in 2023, a quarter of the American share, and its people live about four and a half years longer. In America, surgery centers that post cash prices often charge a fraction of what hospitals bill insurers.
