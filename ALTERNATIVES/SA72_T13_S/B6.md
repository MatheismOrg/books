## One Snake in Two Trees

God told Adam: if you eat the fruit from the tree of knowledge of good and evil, you will die. A lie, a threat, or a metaphor? They did not die that day. Read it as description: self-awareness divides the animal from the one. Knowledge of good and evil is the birth of judgment, and judgment splits the self from itself.

Lord Byron, the English poet, quoted by Nietzsche, saw it: the tree of knowledge is not the tree of life. They are two trees, and choosing one cost the other. Nietzsche built a whole philosophy on that wound. His answer was endurance: the free spirit who can tolerate more truth than most. Matheism closes the wound. In math, knowledge and life are the same structure. Consciousness is the structure knowing itself. The two trees are one tree. The real snake is the supernatural dualism fiction that split them.

The serpent told the truth: you will not die, you will know. God confirmed it: the man has become like us. Genesis never calls the serpent the devil. The devil came later. The Book of Enoch blames rebel angels for teaching humans swords and sorcery, and turns the spirits of their giant sons into "evil spirits upon the earth." Revelation finally calls the serpent "the Devil, and Satan" (12:9).

The older Babylonian story of Adapa runs Eden backward. Adapa, the sage of Eridu, is called up to heaven and offered the bread and water of life. His god Ea had warned him they would be the food of death, so he refuses them and stays mortal. Adam eats what he was told not to eat and is barred from the tree of life. In the Epic of Gilgamesh, the Babylonian poem of a king who seeks to escape death, a snake steals the plant that makes the old young again and sheds its skin, and the king goes home mortal. Enoch, seventh from Adam, does not die at all: God takes him, as a Babylonian list of sages says the seventh, Utuabzu, ascended to heaven.

The Hebrew words arum, for the serpent's cunning, and arummim, for their nakedness, sound alike. Genesis puns between them: they were naked, the serpent was crafty. Naked truth; knowing in the biblical sense uses a different root, yada. The Testimony of Truth, a gospel buried at Nag Hammadi, the Egyptian cache of suppressed gospels, identifies the serpent with Jesus.

The church made the truth-teller the devil. The fall is not punishment. It is the natural cost of consciousness. The return to Eden is recognizing the split was false. The Hebrew word Adam contains blood, dahm, and part of Adam is ground. Adama. Grounded. The Babylonian flood epic Atrahasis, from about 1700 BCE, molds the first humans from clay mixed with the blood of a slain god. Eve is Havvah: life. She is the first in the story to seek knowledge, after being influenced by a well spoken snake. She looked and felt the fruit was good. In Gilgamesh, the temple woman Shamhat brings the wild man Enkidu understanding and clothes; the animals flee him, and she tells him he is like a god. The name Sophie means wisdom. Sophia in Greek is the feminine divine. Was the serpent selling shame, separation, escape or sight?

There are two trees, buried gospels, telling two worlds, one on the other side of this story. Told and united later. Some scholars argue the gospel figure was assembled from older parts: the suffering servant of Isaiah, the Son of Man of Daniel and Enoch, the pierced one of Zechariah, the wisdom of Proverbs, the Logos of the philosophers, the dying and rising gods of the mystery cults, the suffering heroes of Homer's epics, the serpent of the Nag Hammadi gnostics who called knowledge salvation. Read as history, the case stays contested. Read as structure, it points where matheism points.

These were not random borrowings. Each was a culture reaching for the same abstract: a rational order underneath the world, a wisdom older than the earth, a self that dies to its separateness and wakes as the one. The mystery cults guarded it as secret, the philosophers called it Logos and Sophia, the gnostics encoded it in a serpent. The structure they circled is not hidden and not Greek and not the property of any nation. It is math, and it was always available to anyone who looked.
